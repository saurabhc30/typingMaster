How to Increase Typing Speed: 10 Practical Ways to Type Faster

Faster typing is not simply about hitting more keys per second. The biggest improvements usually come from better finger movement, fewer mistakes, and practice that targets the parts of typing slowing you down.

TypingMeter Guide • 8 min read • Typing Practice
The short answer

To increase typing speed, start by making your keystrokes accurate and consistent. Then practice touch-typing movements, identify the keys that cause the most errors, and use short focused sessions to train those weaknesses. Measure both WPM and accuracy instead of chasing speed alone.

If you have ever taken a typing test and watched your WPM stay around the same number for weeks, you are not alone. Typing speed often improves quickly at first and then becomes harder to move. The reason is that simply typing more does not necessarily train the skill that is limiting your performance.

Someone might need better finger positioning. Another person may type quickly but make enough mistakes that corrections slow the final result. Someone else may know the keyboard well but lose time when unfamiliar words, punctuation, or numbers appear.

This guide takes a more practical approach. Instead of telling you to “practice more,” it shows how to structure practice so that each session has a specific purpose.

What actually improves typing speed?

Typing speed is the result of several skills working together: knowing where keys are, moving your fingers efficiently, recognizing words quickly, maintaining accuracy, and avoiding unnecessary pauses.

That means a good training plan should not treat WPM as the only thing worth improving. If your speed increases while your error rate also rises, the number on the test may look better while your real-world typing becomes less efficient.

01

Measure your starting point before changing your technique

Before trying to improve your WPM, take a normal typing test without changing how you type. Record your speed, accuracy, errors, and how comfortable the test felt.

This gives you a baseline. Without one, it is easy to practice for several days and have no objective way to tell whether the change helped.

Use a consistent test length when comparing results. A 15-second sprint and a five-minute test measure somewhat different things, so avoid comparing them as if they were identical.

02

Build accuracy before aggressively chasing speed

One of the most common mistakes in typing practice is trying to type as fast as possible from the first second.

Instead, slow down enough to make the correct keystroke automatic. Once accuracy becomes stable, increase your pace gradually.

Think of it as building a reliable movement pattern. Speed can be added later; repeatedly practicing incorrect movements is much harder to undo.

Useful rule: If increasing your speed causes your accuracy to collapse, reduce the pace and rebuild control before pushing faster again.
03

Stop looking down at the keyboard

Looking at the keyboard provides immediate visual feedback, but it also interrupts your connection between the text on screen and your fingers.

If you currently look down frequently, do not expect yourself to stop instantly. Start with short passages and deliberately keep your eyes on the text.

You will probably make more mistakes initially. That is normal. The goal is to gradually build a stronger mental map of the keyboard.

04

Use consistent finger positions

Touch typing works best when your fingers have predictable starting positions and each hand handles a consistent group of keys.

You do not need to force an unnatural technique just because somebody else uses it. However, if your fingers constantly cross large distances or you rely on one or two fingers for most letters, experimenting with a more consistent finger pattern can reduce unnecessary movement.

Focus first on comfort and repeatability. A technique that is theoretically efficient but uncomfortable will be difficult to practice consistently.

05

Practice the keys and combinations that slow you down

General typing tests are useful for measuring performance, but they do not always fix the specific reason you are slow.

Pay attention to recurring mistakes. Maybe certain letters are frequently mistyped, certain two-letter combinations cause a pause, or your hands become less coordinated when punctuation appears.

Once you identify a weakness, spend a few minutes practicing it directly instead of completing another random test.

Do not overtrain one weakness. Targeted drills are useful, but combine them with normal passages so the skill transfers back into real typing.
06

Practice words instead of only individual letters

Real typing is not a sequence of isolated letters. Your brain recognizes familiar words and patterns, allowing your fingers to move through common combinations more naturally.

Once basic key familiarity is established, practice normal words and sentences. This helps connect keyboard movement with the language patterns you actually type.

It can also reveal a different kind of problem: you may know where every key is but still hesitate between words.

07

Use short, focused practice sessions

You do not need to spend an hour every day taking typing tests. A shorter session with a clear objective can be easier to maintain and easier to evaluate.

For example, one session could focus on accuracy, another on difficult key combinations, and another on maintaining a steady pace during a longer passage.

The important part is consistency. A repeatable routine gives you more opportunities to learn from mistakes than occasional marathon sessions.

A simple 15-minute typing practice routine

If you are unsure what to do during practice, try this structure. It is deliberately simple so that you can repeat it without needing a complicated training system.

15-minute routine

Adjust the timing if you are a beginner or have more time.

2 minutes
Easy warm-up. Focus on relaxed hands and accurate keystrokes.
4 minutes
Practice difficult letters, words, or combinations that repeatedly cause mistakes.
4 minutes
Type normal passages at a controlled pace. Keep accuracy high.
3 minutes
Take a timed test and record WPM and accuracy.
2 minutes
Review the result. Identify one specific thing to improve next session.
08

Learn to maintain a steady rhythm

Very fast typing is not necessarily frantic typing. Experienced typists often maintain a relatively consistent flow instead of repeatedly accelerating and stopping.

During practice, notice where you hesitate. Do you pause before unfamiliar words? Do you speed up on easy sections and then suddenly stop at punctuation?

Try to make your typing more even. A controlled rhythm can make your performance feel easier while reducing sudden bursts of errors.

09

Increase difficulty gradually

Once basic text feels comfortable, introduce harder material instead of immediately trying to maximize your WPM.

You can progress from common words to longer sentences, less familiar vocabulary, capitalization, punctuation, numbers, or other patterns that resemble the typing you actually do.

Difficulty should challenge you without making every session feel like a struggle. If accuracy falls dramatically, step back slightly and rebuild control.

10

Track progress over time instead of judging one test

Typing performance naturally varies from test to test. Your result can change because of the passage, concentration, fatigue, keyboard, or simply how comfortable you feel that day.

That is why one unusually high or low WPM should not determine whether your training is working.

Look for a trend across multiple sessions. If your typical result is gradually improving while accuracy remains stable, your practice is probably moving in the right direction.

Why accuracy matters more than a higher WPM number

Imagine two people who both record 70 WPM. The first person types accurately and rarely needs corrections. The second person makes frequent mistakes and spends time going back to fix them.

Their test numbers may look similar, but their real typing experience can be very different.

Accuracy is therefore not just a secondary statistic. It is part of the efficiency equation. A useful goal is to increase speed while keeping your error rate under control.

Better goal: Don't aim for the highest WPM you can produce for a few seconds. Aim for the fastest pace you can maintain accurately and consistently.

What to do when your typing speed stops improving

A plateau does not necessarily mean that you have reached your limit. It can mean that your current practice is no longer challenging the skill that needs improvement.

If your accuracy is low

Reduce your speed and concentrate on clean keystrokes. Look for recurring mistakes rather than trying to compensate by typing faster.

If your accuracy is good but WPM is stuck

Examine hesitation and rhythm. You may be accurate but spending too much time pausing between words or combinations. Gradually introduce faster controlled passages.

If certain letters repeatedly cause mistakes

Use targeted drills for those letters and combinations. Then return to normal sentences so the improvement becomes part of natural typing.

If practice has become boring

Change the type of material you use. Mix ordinary passages with focused drills, longer tests, and text containing punctuation or numbers when those skills are relevant to your everyday work.

Your typing setup can affect consistency

Technique is not the only factor. Your physical setup can also affect how comfortable it is to practice for longer periods.

  • Keep your wrists and hands in a comfortable position.
  • Avoid unnecessary tension in your shoulders and fingers.
  • Use a keyboard that feels comfortable and predictable.
  • Take breaks rather than continuing through discomfort.
  • Keep your screen positioned so you can read the text easily.

You do not need expensive equipment to improve your typing speed. Consistent technique and focused practice are much more important than buying a particular keyboard.

How quickly can you improve your typing speed?

There is no universal timeline for reaching a particular WPM. Someone starting from 20 WPM has a different path from someone already typing at 60 WPM.

Your starting technique, practice frequency, accuracy, familiarity with touch typing, and the type of text you practice can all influence the result.

A better way to think about progress is to compare your current typical performance with your previous typical performance. Gradual improvement is more meaningful than a single record score.

How to measure your improvement

Use the same general testing conditions when comparing results. For example, if you normally use a one-minute test, continue using a one-minute test when checking your progress.

Track at least these three things:

  1. WPM — your typing speed.
  2. Accuracy — how consistently you type the intended text.
  3. Errors — where your typing breaks down.

WPM tells you how fast you are moving. Accuracy and errors help explain whether that speed is reliable.

The most effective way to type faster

Improving typing speed is less about forcing your fingers to move faster and more about removing the things that slow them down.

Start with a baseline. Improve accuracy. Learn consistent finger movements. Practice the combinations that cause problems. Use short focused sessions, gradually increase difficulty, and measure your progress using both speed and accuracy.

Most importantly, give the process enough time. Typing is a motor skill, and reliable improvement comes from repeatedly performing the right movements rather than simply taking more tests.

Frequently asked questions

How can I increase my typing speed?

Improve technique first, practice accuracy, identify your recurring weak keys or combinations, and use short focused practice sessions. Once your accuracy is stable, gradually increase your typing pace.

Should I focus on speed or accuracy?

Accuracy should usually come first. If you repeatedly make mistakes while trying to type faster, you may be practicing inefficient movements. Build reliable keystrokes and then increase speed gradually.

How long does it take to improve typing speed?

It varies from person to person. Starting skill, technique, practice quality, consistency, and the type of text you use can all affect progress. Several weeks of consistent practice can reveal meaningful changes for some learners, but there is no universal timeline.

Is touch typing necessary to reach a high WPM?

No particular technique guarantees a specific WPM. However, touch typing can help many people develop consistent finger movements without repeatedly looking down at the keyboard.

Why is my typing speed not improving?

A plateau can happen when practice is too repetitive, accuracy is being ignored, the same weak areas are not being trained, or sessions lack a clear goal. Analyze your errors and change your practice around the specific problem.

Is 10 minutes of typing practice enough?

Ten focused minutes can be useful, particularly when practiced consistently. A short session with a specific goal can be more productive than a much longer unfocused session.
