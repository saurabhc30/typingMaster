Why test both?

WPM measures how quickly you type, while accuracy shows how reliably you enter the intended text. Looking at both numbers gives you a more useful picture of typing performance than speed alone.

A typing speed test can produce an impressive number in seconds, but that number does not tell the whole story. If you type quickly while making frequent mistakes, your actual performance may be less efficient than your WPM suggests.

That is why typing accuracy is worth measuring alongside speed. When you know both numbers, you can tell whether you are actually becoming a more efficient typist or simply pushing yourself to type faster.

What is a typing speed test with accuracy?

A typing speed test with accuracy measures two related skills at the same time: how quickly you type and how accurately you reproduce the text shown on the screen.

During a test, you type a passage for a selected amount of time. Your keystrokes can then be used to calculate performance metrics such as words per minute, accuracy, characters, errors, raw speed, and consistency.

The combination matters because typing speed without accuracy can give an incomplete picture of your actual performance.

WPM vs. accuracy: which matters more?

Neither number should be viewed completely by itself.

WPM tells you how fast you can produce text. Accuracy tells you how reliably you produce it. For everyday typing, school, office work, writing, coding, and communication, maintaining a reasonable speed without constantly correcting mistakes can be more useful than producing the highest possible WPM.

A practical goal: Aim for the fastest pace you can maintain with reliable accuracy instead of chasing a record speed for a few seconds.

Why a higher WPM does not always mean better typing

Consider two hypothetical results:

Typist WPM Accuracy Interpretation
Typist A 70 98% Fast and relatively consistent.
Typist B 78 86% Higher pace but considerably more mistakes.

Typist B has the higher WPM, but that does not automatically make B the more efficient typist. Accuracy provides important context for understanding the result.

How typing accuracy works

Typing accuracy is generally based on how much of the typed content matches the expected text. Different typing-test systems can calculate the score differently, so results from separate websites should not always be treated as directly interchangeable.

When tracking your own progress, the most useful approach is consistency. Compare similar tests on the same platform rather than comparing unrelated scoring systems.

Common reasons typing accuracy drops

Typing too fast
Slow down slightly until correct keystrokes become more consistent.
Looking down
Practice short passages while keeping your eyes on the text.
Weak keys
Identify recurring mistakes and practice those keys directly.
Poor rhythm
Practice an even pace instead of repeatedly accelerating and stopping.
Fatigue
Use shorter focused sessions and take breaks when performance begins to deteriorate.

How to improve typing accuracy

1. Slow down temporarily

If you are making many mistakes, deliberately reduce your pace. The goal is not to type slowly forever. It is to give yourself enough control to practice correct movements.

2. Find recurring mistakes

Look for patterns instead of treating every error as unrelated. You may repeatedly miss the same key, confuse two letters, or make mistakes when changing between hands.

3. Practice the weak area

Once you know what causes problems, spend a few minutes practicing that specific area before returning to normal passages.

4. Increase speed gradually

When your accuracy becomes more stable, increase your pace in small steps. If accuracy suddenly falls, reduce the pace again.

What typing test length should you use?

Different test lengths are useful for different goals.

Test length Useful for
15–30 seconds Quick checks and short speed practice.
60 seconds A convenient general-purpose benchmark.
2 minutes Testing whether you can maintain your pace.
5 minutes Longer sustained typing and consistency practice.

When comparing your progress, use similar test lengths. A short sprint and a long typing session measure different aspects of performance.

Why consistency matters

Your highest typing score is not necessarily your most useful score.

A single excellent result can happen because the passage contained familiar words or because you were unusually focused. Long-term progress is better judged by your normal performance across multiple sessions.

Track WPM, accuracy, and errors together. If your typical WPM is increasing while your accuracy remains stable, that is a much more meaningful improvement than a single record score.

For beginners: focus on control first

If you are new to touch typing, do not worry about matching an experienced typist's WPM. Your first goal should be learning where the keys are and developing predictable finger movements.

Start with short tests and simpler passages. As your confidence grows, gradually increase both speed and difficulty.

For experienced typists: find your bottleneck

If you already type quickly, taking more random tests may not solve the problem keeping your WPM from increasing.

  • Do mistakes increase when punctuation appears?
  • Do certain letter combinations cause hesitation?
  • Does your speed fall during longer tests?
  • Do you frequently correct mistakes immediately?
  • Does accuracy drop sharply when you push your WPM?

The answer can tell you what kind of practice you actually need.

Speed and accuracy work together

A typing test becomes much more useful when you look beyond the headline WPM number.

WPM tells you how quickly you type. Accuracy tells you how reliably you type. Errors show where you need work, while consistency helps reveal whether your performance holds up over time.

If your goal is to become a better typist, use all of these signals together. Build accurate movements first, then gradually increase speed while keeping mistakes under control.

Frequently asked questions

Why should I test typing speed and accuracy together?

WPM shows how quickly you type, while accuracy shows how reliably you enter the intended text. Looking at both gives you a more useful picture of your typing performance than speed alone.

What is a good typing accuracy?

There is no single accuracy score that is ideal for every situation. In general, consistently accurate typing is more useful than maximizing WPM while making frequent mistakes.

How can I improve typing accuracy?

Slow down slightly, focus on correct keystrokes, identify recurring mistakes, practice weak keys and combinations, and gradually increase speed after accuracy becomes stable.

Should I focus on WPM or accuracy first?

For most learners, accuracy is a useful foundation. Once you can type consistently with fewer mistakes, gradually increasing speed becomes easier to control.
